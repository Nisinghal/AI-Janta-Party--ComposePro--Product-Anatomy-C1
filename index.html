Why it's different

No filter can move your plate.

Other camera apps dress up a bad photo afterwards. Compose Pro fixes it in real life, before you press the shutter.

How it works

Three things happen, in this order

  1. 1 Detecting…

    Hold still for 3 seconds

    The app finds what you're pointing at and outlines it. It doesn't need a list of objects: a thali, a mug, a scooty and a sofa with a frame above it all work.

  2. 2 Red number → green ✓

    Do the steps

    One to three short steps, most important first. The phone checks most of them itself: its tilt sensor, where the subject sits and how big it is. Can't do one? Tap it to tick it off.

  3. 3 Shutter or Auto shot

    Take the photo

    Tap the shutter whenever you like. Or turn on Auto shot: when every step is green and you hold still, it takes the photo for you in about a second.

What it does

A photographer's eye, in plain words

Finds what you're shooting

It outlines the main thing in view by itself. Things that belong together, like two chairs and the painting above them, are framed as one group.

Steps you can follow

Every step comes from one short list of phrases, so the same picture always gets the same words. A small moving picture shows which way to tip the phone.

Checks your work

A dot on your subject and a circle where it should go. Move until the dot is in the circle and the step turns green. No guessing whether you did it right.

Keeps it straight

A level line follows the object's own edges, like a table or a laptop, so it looks straight in the photo even when the table itself isn't level.

Auto focus on tap

Tap anywhere on the screen and it locks focus there. Useful when the plate isn't centred, or something in front keeps stealing the camera's attention.

Works sideways too

Turn the phone for something long and the steps turn with you. "Left" means your left, and the photo is saved the right way up.

The words it uses

Short phrases you can act on

The AI photographer picks the step; the app writes it, always from this list. No "rule of thirds", no "adjust exposure", and no "tilt" with no direction.

Food first

It knows how each dish wants to be seen

Compose Pro started with food, where good framing is easiest to see. These are the angles it asks for.

What's on the tableAngle it asks forWhy
A thali, pizza, a flat layLook down from aboveEvery dish shows at once, nothing hides behind anything else.
Dal, noodles, a cake sliceLook at it at a slant (about 45°)You see the top, the side and the depth, like sitting at the table.
Chai, lassi, a bottleLook at it straight onTall things look their real shape from their own height.
A busy table behind itRemove the worst thing, or look down moreJars and extra bowls pull the eye away from the food.

Privacy

Your photos stay on your phone

  • Photos are saved to your phone's own gallery. Compose Pro has no account, no sign-in and no cloud album.

  • Only while the camera is open, the camera view is sent to Google Gemini to suggest the steps. Nothing else leaves the phone.

  • Finding the subject, the level line and the step checks all run on the phone itself.

Install

  1. Tap Download for Android on your phone and open the file when it's done.
  2. If the phone asks, let your browser install apps (Settings → Allow from this source), then go back and tap Install.
  3. The app isn't on the Play Store yet, so Play Protect may warn you. Tap More details → Install anyway.
